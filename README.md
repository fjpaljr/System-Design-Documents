# System-Design-Documents

Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?
The client for this project was DriverPass, a company that wanted to help students prepare for their DMV driving tests. They needed a web based system that allowed customers to register accounts, select training packages, book driving lessons, and take online practice tests. The system also needed to support staff roles such as a secretary, IT officer, and owner, each with their own level of access.

What did you do particularly well?
One thing I did particularly well was translating the client's requirements into clear UML diagrams that covered the full scope of the system, from how users interact with it down to how the underlying data is structured.

If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?
If I could revise one part of my work, I would go back and refine the class diagram. I would spend more time thinking through the relationships between classes and make sure the diagram more accurately reflects how the data flows through the system.

How did you interpret the user’s needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user’s needs when designing?
To interpret the user's needs I went back to the interview transcript and business requirements document repeatedly to make sure every design decision was grounded in something the client actually asked for. Considering user needs is critical in system design because a technically sound system that does not match what the user actually needs will ultimately fail regardless of how well it is built.

How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?
My approach to designing software starts with fully understanding the problem before writing anything down. In the future I would continue using techniques like use case analysis and iterative UML diagramming to break down complex requirements into manageable pieces before moving into implementation.
